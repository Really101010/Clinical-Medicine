The most common site of tracheal invasion is <mark style="background-color: #705A16; color: white">at or near Berry’s ligament</mark>, because the thyroid gland is fixed to the trachea by this ligament. The recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) is located lateral to the trachea and runs in under the inferior pharyngeal constrictor muscle (IPCM) just cranial to Berry’s ligament and enters the larynx. Thyroid cancer in this region also often involves the RLN.[^1]
